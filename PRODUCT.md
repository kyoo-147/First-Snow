# Product

## Register

product

## Users

Primary users are children in guided learning flows, especially young learners who benefit from short prompts, visual choices, calm pacing, and emotionally safe interactions.

Secondary users are parents or caregivers who review progress, adjust safety controls, manage routines, and understand what Snow noticed through observational summaries rather than technical or clinical language.

## Product Purpose

Snow is a child-safe AI learning companion with a gentle lesson system, a character-led companion experience, calm routines, and parent controls.

The product exists to make learning and emotional support feel safe, visual, encouraging, and low-pressure for children while giving caregivers clear, calm oversight.

Success means the child experience stays focused, warm, and easy to follow, and the parent experience stays trustworthy, legible, and protective without feeling like an enterprise dashboard.

## Brand Personality

Gentle, playful, reassuring.

Snow should feel warm, child-safe, visually soft, and quietly magical without becoming noisy, chaotic, overly gamified, or medically authoritative.

## Anti-references

- A normal chatbot dashboard where chat history is the primary surface
- Neon dark-mode AI companion products
- Technical settings-heavy child screens
- Clinical or diagnostic therapy interfaces
- Generic SaaS admin dashboards with flat cards and cold enterprise language
- Romantic, anime-companion, or adult-coded VTuber aesthetics

## Design Principles

1. Stage-first, not chat-first.
2. One primary task per child screen.
3. Parent trust comes from clarity, calm language, and obvious safeguards.
4. Visual softness should support focus, not create clutter.
5. Reuse governance patterns and tokens before inventing new UI language.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Target WCAG AA as a baseline across child and parent surfaces.

Child-facing UI should prefer 16px minimum body text, larger prompts, clear 44px minimum touch targets, low cognitive load, and calm motion only.

The interface should support neurodiverse learners through simple flows, limited simultaneous choices, supportive copy, visible safety actions, and reduced reliance on dense text or color-only status signals.
